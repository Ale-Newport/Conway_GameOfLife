### Emergent Civilizations — executed rules

**Time.** One tick is one hour, a day 24 ticks. Demography is compressed: a "year" of life is 240 ticks. Founders are 16–35 years old, adults are 14+, fertile until 45, and die of old age at 55–75 years.

**World.** Tiles are grassland, forest (wood), hills (stone), water (fish; impassable, drinkable from the shore), fertile riverbank, berry bushes (food) or mountain (impassable). Wild stocks regrow linearly up to capacity, `stock ← min(cap, stock + α·richness·multiplier)` (Sugarscape G_α; α = 0.02 berries, 0.012 fish, 0.01 wood, 0.003 stone). Walking costs `c = c_terrain·(1 − k·I)` (grass 1, berries 1.2, forest 1.6, hills 2), with trail intensity `I = G/100`: entering a tile adds `1 − G/100` to `G` and trails fade as `G ← G·(1 − 1/T)` (active-walker trails). Tiles with `I ≥` road threshold are roads. Agents walk `1/c` tiles per tick along A* routes (steering *arrive*, sub-stepped).

**Needs.** Energy `E` burns `metabolism·(1 + effort)` per tick (effort 0.6 working, 0.3 walking, −0.3 resting); one food unit gives 0.25 energy; hunger is `1 − E`. Thirst (×1.3 while working), fatigue (×1.5 working, ×1.2 walking) and loneliness (0.004/tick) grow linearly; drinking at a shore (land touching water) removes 0.25 thirst per tick, resting 0.04 fatigue (0.07 at home). Perceived danger relaxes towards the local threat (recent fights within 2 tiles, +0.15 in foreign territory, damped by `1 − 0.6·risk`). Health falls by 0.012/tick at zero energy and 0.015/tick at saturated thirst, recovers 0.003/tick when fed and watered; death at zero health. Carried food spoils at 0.1 %/tick, stored food at 0.05 %/tick.

**Decisions (utility AI).** Agents re-decide when a goal ends or is unreachable, when thirst or hunger ≥ 0.8 pre-empts it (a search for an unknown water or food source is left to finish), and otherwise 8 ticks after the expected arrival. Only places and agents in the same connected land region are considered. Every feasible action is scored as the product of response-curve considerations (Dave Mark's compensation factor) times an action weight; the current goal gets ×1.15 and scores get ±5 % noise. Actions: eat, drink, rest (at home if possible), forage/fish, log, quarry, plant/harvest, craft, build (hut, farm plot, group storage), trade, socialise, share food, store food, steal, court, explore. The need to gather a good is the largest of its absolute marginal utility `(m_g/(h_g + 1))/max_k m_k` (Cobb–Douglas weights `m`, holdings `h` including the food in the household store, so needs fall as holdings grow and gathering saturates), for food the reserve shortfall and hunger, the missing share of the current building plan's cost, and the refill of goods others have been buying (learned demand × trade propensity); planting also weighs the food need. Without food, foraging is scored as survival, and critical thirst outranks everything regardless of distance. Agents remember shores and resource patches (≥ 2 units) beyond vision, share this knowledge when talking or trading, and search beyond vision in a correlated random walk when water or food is unknown.

**Production.** Productivity = `(0.5 + skill)·(1 + tool bonus if carrying tools)·(0.5 for children)·(0.6 + 0.4·health)`. Gathering takes `1·productivity` per tick (carry limit 24); gatherers of one tile share its stock proportionally. Crafting turns one wood and one stone into one tool at `0.08·productivity` per tick; work wears tools by 0.01 per tick. Planting costs 0.5 food; crops grow `fertility·crop multiplier/96` per tick (riverbank 1, grassland 0.6), yield `10·fertility·(0.5 + planter's farming skill)`, are harvested at `3·productivity` per tick and rot 120 ticks after ripening. Buildings cost materials when laid out (hut 6 wood + 3 stone, storage 8 wood + 6 stone, farm plot 2 wood) and need `work` units (2, 3, 0.8) at `0.06·productivity` per tick; group members may help. Skills learn by doing, `s ← s + λ(1 − s)`, and fade otherwise, `s ← s(1 − φ)`.

**Barter (Sugarscape T).** Welfare `W = Π (h_g + 1)^{m_g/m_T}` over food, wood, stone and tools; demand weights `m` come from metabolism, building plans and worn tools. For each pair of goods the agent with the higher `MRS = (m_a/(h_a+1))/(m_b/(h_b+1))` buys good `a` at price `p = √(MRS_A·MRS_B)` in steps of 0.5 units, executed only while both welfares rise and the MRSs do not cross. Perceived adults whose MRS gap `1 − e^{−|Δ ln MRS|}` (over feasible pairs) is ≥ 0.25 are candidates; for the four best (gap discounted by distance) the meeting is simulated and valued by its expected welfare gain, `1 − e^{−Δ ln W/0.05}`, and the agent walks to the best partner worth ≥ 0.25 (a gain of about 1.4 %). Partners are matched one-to-one; a meeting allows `4 + 8·mean trading skill` steps. Sellers raise their stock for sale by what they sold (at most 12 per good, fading 3 %/day; the bulk goods together are scaled to fill at most half the carrying capacity); both remember the place as their market and carry goods for sale there when nobody is in sight.

**Society.** Affinity in `[−1, 1]` towards at most `max_relationships` known agents: +0.002·cooperation per tick within 2 tiles, socialising +0.04·c_i·c_j per tick, trade +0.04·cooperation, gifts +0.15 (receiver) / +0.05 (giver), theft −0.5 (victim), −0.1 (thief), −0.2 (witnessing group members), fights −0.3, kin +0.6 at birth; affinities shrink by 2 % a day. Groups are connected components (≥ 3 agents) of mutual affinity ≥ bond threshold (half of it keeps current members together); identity follows the largest member overlap, so groups persist, split and merge. Cooperative agents give `min(3, food − reserve/2)` to hungry group members and kin. Agents keep their reserve (`2 + 6·(1 − time preference)`) or, if larger, their food for sale on them and put food above it in their group's store (scored by cooperation) or in their own hut, the household store (scored by how full their load is, which frees capacity for materials and harvests); group members eat from group stores and residents from their hut.

**Conflict.** A hungry adult (hunger > 0.55, food < 0.5) may steal up to 3 food from a non-kin agent holding ≥ 1.5 (from its own group only if its cooperation < 0.5); theft is scored by desperation, `1 − cooperation`, risk, the victim's food (discounted by affinity) and its convenience `d_food/(d_food + d_victim)` against the nearest known food. Adult victims resist with probability `0.2 + 0.6·risk`. Group members within 5 tiles of someone gathering in their territory confront them with probability `0.15·(1 − cooperation)·(0.3 + 0.7·risk)` per tick (×1.5 on contested tiles); a beaten trespasser drops what it gathered there. Fights are won with probability `s_a/(s_a + s_b)`, `s = (0.5 + health)(0.5 + energy)(1.3 with tools)(0.4 for children)`; the loser loses 0.3 health, the winner 0.08, and danger marks the place.

**Territory.** Every day each finished building of a group projects influence `4·w·(1 + 0.25·ln members) − falloff·distance` (w: hut 1, storage 1.6, farm 0.6); a tile belongs to the group with the largest positive influence and is contested when another group's influence is positive and within 1.

**Births and deaths.** An adult courting a partner of the other sex within reach has a child with it when both are fertile, have energy ≥ 0.6, carry ≥ 3 food, have mutual affinity ≥ 0.2, are not parent and child and had no child for 2 years (each gives 3 food); the child's preferences are the parents' mean plus N(0, 0.05) noise. The dead leave their goods to their living children and buildings to the eldest; buildings without owner and group decay over 1000 ticks.
